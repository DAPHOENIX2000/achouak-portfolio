The Universal Case Method

Every case on this exam — whatever the topic — yields to the same six-move sequence. Memorise the moves, not the cases.

✓ Reusable moves that earn points in almost any case

  • Put public welfare first. When any duty conflicts with employer loyalty, public safety, health, and welfare win — say it explicitly.
  • Name both kinds of justice. Distributive (benefits vs. burdens) and procedural (was the process fair, informed, participatory).
  • Diagnose failure at three levels. Personal, cognitive, organizational barriers — then fix each.
  • Count the externalities. Pollution, displacement, long-term and irreversible effects off the balance sheet.
  • Always end with prevention. Personal + organizational + institutional causes, a policy with owners and triggers, and how you'd implement it.
  • Drop the vocabulary: risk = probability × loss, informed consent, data minimization, due care, FFP, duty of care, digital divide.

✗ Traps that lose points

  • "It's technically fine, so it's acceptable." Feasibility never equals acceptability.
  • Choosing a side before weighing the conflicting values.
  • Stopping at "report it" without naming the channel and the fallback (escalate to the regulator).
  • Forgetting the people with no voice — usually the point of the case.
  • Ending with no prevention or policy. A case answer that doesn't look forward is incomplete.

One-line memory hook

  • F-S-C-P-D-P: Facts → Stakeholders → Conflict → Principles → Decision → Prevention.

The 6-line answer skeleton (write this every time)

  • Line 1 — Conflict: "This case pits ___ against ___."
  • Line 2 — Facts & roles: what happened; who holds knowledge/power; what's hidden.
  • Line 3 — Stakeholders: who benefits, who bears the burden, who has no voice.
  • Line 4 — Principles: name the framework(s) with exact vocabulary.
  • Line 5 — Decision: the defensible action + fallback (regulator) + public welfare first.
  • Line 6 — Prevention: causes at 3 levels + one enforceable policy with an owner.
