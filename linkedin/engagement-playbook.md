# Daily Engagement Playbook

**This matters more than the posts.** Comments on other people's posts drive more
inbound than your own content, especially under 5,000 followers. Posting is five
days a week. This is seven.

## The daily 30 minutes

| Block | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Morning, 15 min | Right after you post, or 8:00 AM on non-posting days | 8-12 comments on fresh posts (under 60 minutes old) from your peer tier |
| Evening, 10 min | Around 9:00 PM Shenzhen | 4-6 comments, plus reply to every comment on your own posts |
| Anytime, 5 min | — | Reply to anyone who replied to you. The 6-24h window after an author replies is where threads actually convert |

Target: **10-20 substantive comments a day.** Substantive means 200-350
characters, two short paragraphs, one concrete thing.

## The 70/20/10 split

Of every ten comments:

- **7 to peers** — photographers, stylists, models, casting directors, agency
  creatives, other people building in Shenzhen. This is the referral network and
  it is the one that actually books you work.
- **2 to aspirational accounts** — creative directors at brands, AI-media
  founders, virtual production leads, people with 20k+ followers in the
  generative-media conversation. One reply from one of them puts you in front of
  their whole audience.
- **1 to prospects** — brand marketing people at companies you want to shoot for.
  Never pitch. Just be visibly intelligent in their comments for six weeks.

## How to write the comment

From the voice rules, non-negotiable:

- 200-350 characters. Two short paragraphs, line break between.
- **At least one noun or concept that is not already in the post.** LinkedIn's
  ranker checks this. If your comment only restates their thesis, it is invisible.
- One concrete number or named entity, minimum.
- One line that could be screenshotted alone.
- End with a specific question or a clean landing. Never "What do you think?"

**Never:** "Great insight!", "100%", "This.", "Couldn't agree more", "Love this."
These are worse than not commenting; they attach your name to nothing.

## Your three comment angles

You have an unfair advantage: you can answer from two sides of any AI-and-image
post. Rotate these.

1. **The technical correction, gently.** Someone posts about AI models replacing
   photographers. You know what diffusion actually does. Correct one specific
   thing, without condescending. This is the highest-value comment you can leave
   and almost nobody else in the thread can leave it.

2. **The view from inside the frame.** Someone posts about AI in fashion from the
   marketing side. You have stood in the room they are theorising about. "I was on
   a set in Shenzhen last month where..." — one concrete scene beats any opinion.

3. **The between-worlds read.** Someone posts about a market, a culture, a
   creative scene. You have Morocco, China, and four languages. Offer the thing
   only that vantage gives, briefly.

## Where to find the posts

Build these three feeds and work them in order:

1. **A LinkedIn list of 30-40 peers.** Follow, turn on the bell for the ten who
   post most. These are your first-commenter targets.
2. **Hashtag and search feeds:** `#fashionphotography`, `#creativedirection`,
   `#generativeai`, `#virtualproduction`, `#aifashion`, plus searches for
   "AI model", "digital twin", "likeness licensing".
3. **The comment sections of your aspirational accounts.** The other smart
   commenters there are your peer tier, and they are already warmed up.

## First-commenter advantage

A comment on a post under 60 minutes old, from someone with a relevant profile,
gets disproportionate visibility because it sits at the top as the post's reach
compounds. Ten first comments a week on the right accounts is worth more than a
hundred late ones.

## Weekly review

Every Sunday, ten minutes:

- Which comments got an author reply? Those accounts move up the list.
- Which got replies from *other* commenters? That is the strongest signal there is.
- Any thread where an author replied and you did not follow up inside 24 hours?
  Fix that first next week.
- Are you actually at 70/20/10, or did you spend the week talking to the same
  five people?

Once an Apify token is set up, `linkedin-thread-monitor` does this review
automatically and tells you which threads are warm. Until then it is manual and
still worth the ten minutes.
