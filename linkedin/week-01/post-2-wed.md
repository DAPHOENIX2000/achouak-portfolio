## Draft

> In 2018 I was writing sorting algorithms in a lecture hall in Morocco.
>
> In 2023 I landed in Shenzhen with four languages and nobody's phone number.
>
> The two years between them were spent trying to choose. I first stepped in front of a camera in 2021, and from that day everyone had the same advice: pick one. Computer science or the camera.
>
> I took the advice seriously. I would introduce myself as a developer who also models, or a model with a technical background, and I would watch the sentence lose the room halfway through.
>
> What changed was not a skill. I did not get better at posing and I did not get better at code.
>
> I stopped putting an apology in the middle of the introduction.
>
> That apology was the thing costing me work. A casting director does not want half an engineer. A studio does not want someone who codes as a hobby. They want a person who knows exactly what he is.
>
> Now I say it in one line, and the line does not bend: I work at the seam between machine systems and human image-making.
>
> Same person as 2018. Same two skills. Different sentence.
>
> ..
>
> Identity is not a fixed point. It is a direction of travel.
>
> What did you stop apologising for, and what did it change?
