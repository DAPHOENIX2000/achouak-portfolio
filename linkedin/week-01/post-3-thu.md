## Draft

> Diffusion models, explained to a ten-year-old.
>
> This is the machine making the AI models in your feed. Most people working in fashion have never had it explained without math.
>
> 🌫 NOISE: it starts with a screen of pure static. No image. Nothing.
>
> 🔙 THE TRICK: it was trained by watching millions of photographs get slowly destroyed into static, one step at a time. Then it learned to run that destruction backwards.
>
> 🎯 THE PROMPT: your words decide which photograph it un-destroys toward. "Moroccan man, 25, hard side light, film grain."
>
> 🪜 THE STEPS: it clears a little static, looks again, clears a little more. Usually twenty to fifty passes. Each pass the face gets more certain.
>
> 🧠 WHY IT LOOKS RIGHT: it never copies one photograph. It learned the shape of what faces do. Where light breaks on a cheekbone, how fabric folds when a shoulder drops.
>
> 🚫 WHY IT STILL FAILS: hands, reflections, jewellery. Anywhere the rule is geometric instead of statistical. It knows what usually happens. It does not know what must happen.
>
> That last line is the whole thing.
>
> A diffusion model is a machine for what usually happens. A good photographer is a machine for what has never happened.
>
> I studied this before I ever stood in front of a lens, and it is still the most useful thing I know on set.
>
> What is the tell that makes you clock an AI image in under a second?
>
> #AI #Photography
