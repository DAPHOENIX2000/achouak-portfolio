## Read this before you use it

**This is the one post I could not finish for you.**

F11 opens inside the emotional peak of a real scene. It only works if it happened,
and readers punish manufactured stakes harder than they punish bad writing. So
below is the *structure* with your verified facts in place, and four blanks that
only you can fill.

If you would rather not put a hard moment on LinkedIn, skip this post entirely and
run the alternate at the bottom instead. That is a legitimate choice, not a
retreat.

## Draft scaffold

> [[LINE 1 — one short line dropped into the middle of the worst moment. No setup, no scene-setting. Example shape: "The casting director said my name twice and I understood neither time." Yours must be true.]]
>
> [[LINE 2 — what was actually at stake. Money, the visa, the plan, the thing you had told people back home you were doing.]]
>
> I had been in Shenzhen [[weeks / months]]. My Chinese was [[what it actually was]].
>
> [[3-5 short present-tense lines. What the room looked like. What you could hear. What your hands were doing. No adjectives, only what a camera would have recorded.]]
>
> [[THE TURN — what changed, who showed up, what it cost. One person, named or not. A specific act, not a feeling.]]
>
> [[WHAT YOU DID NEXT — the decision, not the lesson.]]
>
> Nobody in Morocco could have prepared me for that room, and nobody in that room knew what I had crossed to get there.
>
> ..
>
> [[ONE LINE of meaning. Not a moral. Let the scene carry it. Your portfolio already has the register: "He doesn't look at the city. He listens to it."]]

## Rules for filling it

- Real story only. If it did not happen, do not write it.
- Specifics in the first two lines: a date, a place, a number, a name. That is the
  whole difference between vulnerability and performance.
- No all-caps first line. Carry the intensity in the word choice.
- No "Plot twist" or "Then everything changed" bridge.
- Do not explain the feeling. Name what happened and stop.
